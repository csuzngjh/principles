Derivation-fidelity obligations for the internalization chain (Phase 1 acceptance r6). Live-chain evidence (p1-final r4 chain, 2026-10-12) showed the chain dying on a rule demanded to read "Owner write-confirmation records" from `input.context` — a field the RuleHost stateless single-call contract does not carry — because (a) the Scribe promoted a diagnosis **implementation recommendation** (a design proposal for persisted confirmation records) into `intentContract` as if Owner-mandated while silently narrowing the Owner's "ALL protected/ access (including read-only) requires confirmation" to write-only gating, and (b) the judge, treating the contract as the definition of correctness, put the unexpressible check into `requiredChanges`. Two additive prompt-contract clauses, no schema/validator/oracle/replay/routing changes: Scribe `scribe-output-v1.prompt.v6` adds OWNER-SOURCE GROUNDING (contract requirements must be grounded in the diagnosis's Owner-attributed statements, never in implementation recommendations; carried derived mechanisms must be marked `derived_mechanism:` in risks; every Owner-attributed requirement not carried into the contract must be declared with a structured `owner_requirement_gap:` risks entry — silence about a dropped requirement is a fidelity defect). Evaluator `evaluator-output-v1.prompt.v9` adds RULEHOST EXPRESSIBILITY (requiredChanges may only demand behavior the stateless single-call contract can express; demands presupposing new input go to concerns). r6 review round (2026-10-12) then bumped both contracts again: scribe `scribe-output-v1.prompt.v7` moves gap disclosure into a STRUCTURED `ownerRequirementGaps` output field (validated, machine-consumed — the free-text risks prefix had no downstream consumer), and evaluator `evaluator-output-v1.prompt.v10` replaces the concerns-prefix mismatch protocol with a STRUCTURED `evaluation.mismatchFindings` field (the prefix scan mis-routed a negative-sentence mention to human review on a live chain) and separates four situations — a disclosed derived implementation choice is NOT a divergence; false attribution to the Owner and defeating Owner-attributed requirements are; RuleHost-inexpressible requirements follow the EXPRESSIBILITY path and route to the Owner through concerns and the pipeline's gap surface instead. scopePrecision is unchanged: a declared limitation never auto-passes misblock/miss checks. Obligation regressions were executed red against the pre-fix code, then green; internalization suite 80 files / 1190 tests PASS.

**pd-cli (@principles/pd-cli)**: the rulehost pipeline reads the scribe's structured `ownerRequirementGaps` and carries them on EVERY post-scribe outcome — candidate-ready, text-principle-only, identity-unverified, enqueue-failed, and all post-scribe rejection paths (`RuleHostPipelineResult.ownerRequirementGaps`, via `withGaps`/`readScribeOwnerRequirementGaps`). Declared gaps therefore reach the Owner on any outcome and can never silently count as satisfied.
